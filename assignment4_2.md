## The question
  One question I have that could lead to a story would be: how effective MLB pitchers have been since the “sticky stuff” crack down? 
## 
  This story would cover how the MLB added checks between innings to ensure pitchers were not using illegal substances to have an edge over batters. With the MLB starting their spring training this week, it would be extremely timely and newsworthy as there is available information and data to investigate this change in the rules. 
## What data to use?:
  The MLB has tracked so much data and created many different new statistics about the game they may find to see as an advantage to winning. I would look specifically at pitches thrown from 2020 to 2026. More specifically, splitting the pitches thrown (balls/strikes) into two groups, 2020 to 2023 and 2024 to 2025 showing the rule change years. I would look into the weather on each day the pitcher was throwing in the game because moisture in the air can help with gripping the ball. There is data that looks at the other teams batting ability and ability to get on base. Also, a lot of data on pitch location and ability to get the target each time. 
  Most of this data is accessible to the public online through MLB statistic resources. By narrowing down the data to these seasons and pitchers who have thrown over 10,000 pitches gives a better scope to understanding trends and eliminating less used pitchers.

## Accessing the data:
  Many of the links below offer CSVs to access the data. With these datasets, they can be added to a spreadsheet. In this spreadsheet, they can be cleaned and reorganized in a way that will help the story. Some of the data from the baseball savant website may not be helpful at all to the story. Not worth deleting in the spreadsheet, but highlighting other columns that may be beneficial. An example of this was when I was looking at the MLB.com data. It had a column with strikes thrown per at bat percentage. However, it also had columns with total innings pitched, and completed games do not really have much to do with the topic of pitchers' success with and without gripping substances. Through organizing and making pivot tables, you can begin to see trends like consistency from some players and major statistical change following 2023 from others.
