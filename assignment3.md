# Finding the healthiest cereal
## Unhealthy Cereals
  As you stroll down the breakfast aisle of the local grocery store, the options for cereals can be extremely overwhelming. The bright colors and characters on the box, misleading numbers and greenwashing advertisements, and all sorts of different flavors and shapes. While many are loaded with sugars and saturated fats, it can be difficult to depict the healthy options against the unhealthy choices. 
  *This is what health experts at the FDA had to say about the healthy choices*
  Before diving into the numbers, it is important to first understand what to look for as key healthy indicators in the cereal. Through research from the National Institutes of Health (NIH https://www.niddk.nih.gov/Dictionary/A/added-sugars), low percentages and grams of sugar serve as healthy cereal options. High sugar cereals offer short term energy that lead to sugar crashes after about an hour after consumption. On top of that, high fiber and protein serve as healthy parts of nutrition labels to look out for. The high fiber is helpful with digestion. The proteins provide sustainable energy to last the whole day rather than short term energy in high sugar cereals.
  *Health expert or Doctor discussing benefits of high fiber and high protein breakfast benefit on the day of a consumer*
## Looking at the data
  By looking at a scope of 80 popular grocery store cereals from over five large companies through a dataset from Kaggle can help understand which cereals might offer the healthiest choice. In order to properly find results, filtering out unnecessary information for the study is important to do. While certain amounts of calories, fats, and sodium are important to nutritious cereal, for this story, it will focus on the importance of the sugars, proteins, and fibers. Along with that, each cereal was rated on a 0 to 100 point scale by consumer reports. These ratings will be used to understand the correlation between nutritious cereal and consumer enjoyment. 
## The findings
  The findings from this data showed a variety of results. Beginning with the healthiest choice, “All-Bran with Extra Fiber” sat at the top with the highest consumer rating among the cereals with less than five grams of sugar and more than three grams of protein. Just below it sat “100% Bran”, “All-Bran”,  “Maypo”, and “Special K” which all had ratings higher than 50 points.
